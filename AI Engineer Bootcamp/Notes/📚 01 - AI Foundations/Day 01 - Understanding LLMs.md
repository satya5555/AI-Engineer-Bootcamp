# Sprint 1 – AI Foundations

## Day 1 – Understanding Large Language Models (LLMs)

---

# Concept

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is the broad field of creating systems that perform tasks that normally require human intelligence.

Machine Learning (ML) is a subset of AI where models learn patterns from data instead of relying on manually written rules.

Deep Learning (DL) is a subset of ML that uses neural networks with many layers to learn complex relationships from large datasets.

Large Language Models (LLMs) are deep learning models trained on massive amounts of text to understand and generate human language.

An LLM does not "think" like a human. Its core task is to predict the most likely next token based on the prompt and the conversation context.

---

# Why It Matters

Understanding how an LLM works makes every AI tool easier to use. Whether you're using ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Cursor, or Google AI Studio, the same core principles apply. Better understanding leads to better prompts, better applications, and fewer mistakes.

---

# Key Takeaways

- AI is the broad field; ML, DL, and LLMs are progressively more specialized areas.
    
- LLMs generate responses one token at a time.
    
- The quality of an AI response depends heavily on the quality of the prompt and the available context.
    
- A context window acts like the model's temporary working memory.
    
- Hallucinations happen because the model predicts plausible text rather than verifying facts.
    
- Different models produce different outputs because of differences in training, tuning, safety policies, and capabilities.

# New Terms

### Artificial Intelligence (AI)

Technology that enables machines to perform tasks that usually require human intelligence.

### Machine Learning (ML)

A method where computers learn patterns from data instead of following fixed rules.

### Deep Learning (DL)

A branch of ML that uses neural networks to solve complex problems.

### Large Language Model (LLM)

A deep learning model trained to understand and generate language.

### Prompt

The instruction or question provided to an AI model.

### Prompt Engineering

The practice of designing prompts that consistently produce high-quality outputs.

### Token

The smallest unit of text processed by an AI model. A token can be a whole word, part of a word, punctuation, or another text fragment.

### Context Window

The amount of previous information an AI model can consider while generating a response.

### Temperature

A setting that controls the balance between predictable and creative responses.

### Hallucination

When an AI model generates incorrect or unsupported information that sounds convincing.

# Common Mistakes

- Writing vague prompts.
    
- Assuming AI is always correct.
    
- Ignoring the importance of context.
    
- Believing every model behaves the same.
    
- Not verifying AI-generated information.
    

---

# Best Practices

- Be specific about the task.
    
- Clearly define the desired output.
    
- Provide enough context.
    
- Break complex requests into smaller steps.
    
- Verify important information before using it.
